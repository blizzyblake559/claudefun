# Module 09 — Posting: Music & Fashion

> *You have two of the most visually and culturally rich identity pillars possible. The discipline isn't finding things to post — it's knowing what not to show, and how to frame what you do.*

---

## The Posting Identity

Music producer + fashion/streetwear is a rare combination with natural cultural weight. Most people in this space destroy the mystique by over-explaining both — the "new music coming" post, the outfit-of-the-day caption, the unboxing, the studio vlog. The gap is completely open because almost nobody in this space practices restraint.

Your edge: refuse what everyone else can't resist posting.

The identity that comes through your feed should feel like: **someone deep in the culture who doesn't need to announce it.**

---

## Music — What To Post

### The Studio

**Do:**
- A corner of your setup — one piece of gear, the light, the cables — no explanation
- The waveform on a screen, out of context, no caption
- A time-stamp: 3:41am, nothing else
- Sound — a 10-second clip of something that raises a question rather than answers one. Not the chorus. The strange middle section. The texture. The thing that makes someone ask "what is this."
- A physical artifact: a handwritten note, a chord chart, a scrawled lyric — partial, not full

**Don't:**
- "Been in the studio all week working on something special 🔥"
- "New music coming soon — stay tuned"
- A full session breakdown / studio vlog explaining your process
- Before/after: "Started with this, ended with this" — it closes the gap
- Post-session emotional debrief: how drained you are, how hard you worked
- Tag every piece of gear in the frame

**The test:** Does this post make someone want to hear the music — or does it substitute for hearing it?

---

### Releasing Music

**Do:**
- Let the music arrive with minimal preamble — drop it, or give it 24 hours' notice maximum
- Post the cover art alone, no caption or one word
- Share a sound clip that creates a question — the most interesting 15 seconds, not the hook
- After release: let it live. One post. The music speaks; you don't.
- If it performs well, you don't comment on it performing well

**Don't:**
- The 3-week countdown with daily updates
- "This one is really personal to me" (tell them that by making it personal, not saying it)
- "I've been working on this for X months" — the listener doesn't care about the timeline
- Post streaming numbers, playlist adds, or play counts
- Ask people to share it, add it, save it — the ask signals insecurity
- Respond to every comment on the release post

**Example caption that works:** nothing. Or the release date. Or one line from the track — not the most obvious line.

---

### Your Sound / Musical Identity

**Do:**
- Post what you listen to — a vinyl, an album cover, a track playing on a speaker — no justification
- Reference oblique influences: the thing three steps behind what you actually make
- Let the sounds you post create a picture of your taste without you explaining your taste
- Post music from other artists sparingly and specifically — each one signals something

**Don't:**
- Explain your genre or sound in a post ("I make a mix of X and Y and Z")
- Announce influences directly ("heavily inspired by...")
- Post that you're "working on something different" or "evolving your sound"
- Over-post other artists' music — it dilutes the picture

## Fashion / Streetwear — What To Post

### Fit Posts

**Do:**
- Full body or three-quarter, natural posture — not a posed fashion editorial stance
- Shoot in context: the street, the room, the light — the environment is part of the image
- Let the fit be visible but let your face or the surroundings be the focal point
- Movement over static — mid-stride, turning, looking elsewhere
- Eyes not always at camera — looking away, looking down, cut above the eyes

**Don't:**
- Caption what you're wearing (no brand tags, no item breakdown)
- Post the mirror selfie as the primary fit documentation
- Post every outfit — one a week at most, only when the combination is genuinely worth showing
- Choose a location that makes no sense just to have a background
- Over-edit: heavy grain, blown-out color, extreme processing — it signals you're hiding the fit behind the filter

**The frame question:** Is the setting as interesting as the fit? If not, find a better one or don't post it.

---

### Pieces and Finds

**Do:**
- A flat lay with one deliberate element — not a full outfit spread
- The detail shot: the stitching, the sole, the label, the texture — not the full shoe
- Something interesting on a rack, in a shop, on a table — not necessarily yours yet
- Vintage or archive pieces: context and provenance matter more than the logo
- The unexpected pairing or find — the thing no one else would notice

**Don't:**
- "Just copped" with the receipt or the bag
- The unboxing — the anticipation shot before you open something, the reveal
- "This dropped today" as if you're a resale account
- Post every purchase — most things you buy should never appear on your feed
- Logo front and center as the composition focus

---

### The Aesthetic as a Whole

Your feed's fashion dimension should communicate: **deep familiarity with the culture, zero need to prove it.**

The person who has to show the logo hasn't arrived yet. The person who wears it like clothing — present in the frame but incidental to the picture — has.

**What the feed should feel like:**
- Someone who gets dressed for themselves
- Someone who has been in the culture long enough to know what matters beyond hype
- Someone whose taste is evident without them curating a "here's my taste" post

---

## Music + Fashion Together

These two identities compound when they're held in the same visual language — and they're rare together when done with restraint.

**Do:**
- Post in the overlap: the studio fit, the show outfit (before, not during), the record store run, the session in a room with something interesting on the walls
- Let both identities appear in the same frame without commenting on either
- The post that is both — you in the studio, the fit visible, the setup visible, neither the point — is the highest-value single image you can post

**Don't:**
- Silo them: "music post" then "fashion post" — let them exist in the same world
- Announce the overlap ("my two passions")
- Use one to promote the other ("new music out — wearing [brand]")

## Platform-Specific Notes

### Instagram (main feed)

- 2–4 posts per week maximum; less is better
- Stories: aesthetic only — no talking head updates, no logistics narration
- Reels: short sound clips or visual fragments — not vlogs or tutorials
- Never post behind-the-scenes "making of" content as your primary output

### Instagram (close friends)

- This is where the real disclosure lives — the unguarded session, the reference track you're obsessing over, the fit you're unsure about
- Use it for the inner circle only; don't expand it to 200 people

### X / Twitter

- Oblique observations, not announcements
- Reference something cultural without over-explaining why
- One line, not a thread
- Never post about your music on the day of release as your only platform — let Instagram carry the visual

### TikTok

- Highest-risk for over-disclosure in this space — the format pulls toward explanation, tutorials, "my process" content
- If used: visual, atmospheric, brief — not talking at camera about your music or your style

## The Posting Calendar (rough guide)

| Frequency | Content |
|---|---|
| 1–2x per week | Fit or fragment — music or fashion, one strong image |
| 1x per release | Music release post — cover art, clip, minimal caption |
| As it happens (rare) | Something genuinely interesting that raises a question |
| Never | Countdowns, announcements, number-sharing, process debriefs |
| Always | Dark gaps between posts — at least 2–3 days of nothing |

---

## The Through-Line

Music and fashion are both languages. The person fluent in a language doesn't explain it — they speak it. Every post that explains what you make, what you wear, or why you care about it is you telling the room you're not sure they'll understand.

Trust the language. Post in it. Let the gap do the work.

The feed of someone deep in both worlds should feel like arriving at a party where something interesting is clearly happening — and not quite being able to place what it is.
