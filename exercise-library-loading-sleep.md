## 2. Loading, reps, and progression

Three different "currencies" depending on the bucket the exercise lives in:

### Bucket A — Breathing/positional drills (90/90, side-lying, rock-back, bear, cross connect, low oblique sit)
- **Currency: breaths and quality, never load.** 3–5 slow breaths × 1–2 sets. Inhale through the
  nose into the target zone, full long exhale (mouth), 2–3 s pause at end-exhale.
- **Progression = position difficulty, not weight:** e.g., quadruped rock-back → all-fours reach →
  bear hover. You're done progressing when the drill feels easy and you can find the expansion fast;
  then it's maintenance.

### Bucket B — Cuff/ER and serratus work (band/cable ER, side-lying ER, wall slide, serratus punch)
- **Reps: 2–3 × 12–15, slow** (≈2 s out, 3 s back), ~2–3 RIR. This is tendon/capacity work — the
  stimulus is time under tension at comfortable end-range, not load PRs. **[Probable]**
- **Progression order:** ① add reps (12 → 15) → ② slow the eccentric (3 → 5 s) → ③ add small load →
  ④ add range (work closer to true end-range ER). One variable at a time, every 1–2 weeks.
- **Contract-relax for left ER:** 3–4 cycles of 5–8 s gentle isometric (~10–20% effort) into IR,
  exhale, ease into new ER range; 2–3×/week. Expect most gains in 3–6 weeks — much of it is
  **stretch tolerance**, which is fine; loaded ER work makes it stick. **[Established mechanism]**

### Bucket C — Hypertrophy lifts (all pressing/pulling/lower-body)
Use **double progression** — the simplest scheme that guarantees progressive overload without
forcing load jumps your shoulder isn't ready for:
1. Pick a rep range per exercise: **8–12 for most compounds, 12–15 for isolation/cuff-adjacent
   work** (lateral raises, flyes, serratus pressing).
2. Keep the load fixed; add reps each session until **all sets** hit the top of the range.
3. Then add the smallest increment (2.5–5 lb DBs, one plate on machines) and drop back to the
   bottom of the range. Repeat.
- **Effort:** Phase 1 ≈ 3–4 RIR → Phase 2 ≈ 2–3 → Phases 3–4 ≈ 1–2 RIR on isolation, 2 on compounds.
  Reps left in reserve is the brake that protects the shoulder while volume does the muscle-building.
- **Why reps-first:** hypertrophy is driven by hard sets near failure across ~8–15+ reps; load is
  just one way to progress, and reps-first lets tissue capacity lead. **[Established]**
- **Per-exercise notes:**
  - *Push-up family:* progress feet height → handles/deficit depth → weighted (plate/vest). Treat as
    a real lift, not a warm-up.
  - *Landmine press:* the bar arc means bigger jumps feel fine; still run double progression 8–12.
  - *Rows/pulldowns:* the **reach/stretch phase is half the exercise** — if adding load shortens
    your reach or brings a shrug, the load is too heavy. Quality gate > load.
  - *Lower body:* no restriction — progress normally, just keep the dead-bug "ribs down" rule under
    heavy loads (don't buy reps with lumbar extension).
- **Autoregulation rule (traffic light, from the main doc):** 🟢 progress · 🟡 repeat the same
  load/reps next session · 🔴 drop the variation back one step (e.g., low-incline DB → floor press).

### Dips and typewriter pull-ups specifically
Since they actively bug you: **remove them now** rather than reduce them — both load your exact
sensitive positions (dips: deep extension+IR under full bodyweight; typewriters: end-range
single-side overhead tension). Substitutions that keep the stimulus:
- Dips → **neutral-grip DB floor press + cable pressdowns** (triceps) or close-grip push-ups on handles.
- Typewriter pull-ups → **half-kneeling one-arm pulldown** (unilateral lat stimulus without the
  end-range hang) or chest-supported row.
Re-test in Phase 3–4, symptom-free only, starting with partial range (dips: short range bands/
assisted; typewriters: regular pull-ups first). They are **optional** — nothing about your goal
requires them.

## 3. Sleep

Honest framing first: **direct RCT evidence on sleep *position* for shoulder pain doesn't exist** —
position guidance is cross-sectional association + mechanics. The *strongest* evidence is about
sleep **amount**: sleep loss measurably increases pain sensitivity (hyperalgesia, worse pain
modulation) and impairs muscle protein synthesis, testosterone, and recovery. **[Established]**
So the #1 sleep intervention for your shoulder and your recomp goal is the boring one: **7–9 h,
consistently.** (Athlete sleep-extension studies — Stanford basketball — showed performance gains
with more sleep. **[Probable]**)

### Position guidance (association + mechanics — **[Probable]**, not RCT-proven)
- **Best default: back-sleeping, arms at your sides or on your torso.** Associated with lowest
  rotator-cuff pain likelihood in cross-sectional data; avoid arms overhead or behind the head
  (both were associated with more cuff pain).
- **If you side-sleep (most people can't change):** lie on the **non-painful side** — for you that
  currently means **avoid the left side** when it's irritable; people with unilateral shoulder pain
  disproportionately sleep on the painful side (~67% in one study — association, causality unclear).
  **Hug a full-length pillow** so the top arm rests on it instead of falling across your chest into
  internal rotation + adduction (the irritable position).
- **Avoid stomach sleeping:** it pins both shoulders into protraction/IR *and* forces sustained
  cervical rotation — bad for both your shoulder and your neck-tightness history. **[Probable, mechanistic]**
- **Right-side-lying caveat for you:** your stiff right rib zone gets compressed lying on it all
  night. If the right side feels *more* locked in the morning, that's a signal — bias back-sleeping
  or left-side (when the left shoulder is quiet) with the pillow hug.

### Pillow (neck-tightness history)
- Goal: **neutral cervical spine** — ears over shoulders, no side-bend.
- **Side-sleeping:** higher loft (~4–6") to bridge the shoulder-to-ear gap.
- **Back-sleeping:** lower loft (~3–5") or a cervical-contour pillow; too high pushes the head into
  flexion and re-feeds neck tone.
- Stable fills (memory foam, latex, buckwheat) hold loft better than feather. **[Probable]**

### Thorax-model angle (clearly labeled **[Theory/Speculative]**)
No formal Cupples/PRI sleep protocol exists. Extrapolating from the "stack" model: avoid setups
that park you in extension + rib flare all night (e.g., very high pillow stacks under the upper
back, stomach lying). **Supine with a pillow under the knees** reduces lumbar extension and lets
the ribs settle — consistent with the model and harmless, so a reasonable default. Cupples notes
the rib cage widens side-to-side supine and deepens front-to-back in side-lying — interesting, but
not a prescription.
