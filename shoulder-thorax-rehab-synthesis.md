# Shoulder/Thorax Rehab & Return-to-Lifting: Evidence-Informed Synthesis

*Educational synthesis, not a diagnosis. See "Red flags" before acting on any of this.
Built from a 5-angle research review; each claim is labeled by confidence:
**[Established]** = strong/consistent evidence · **[Probable]** = reasonable evidence or sound
mechanism · **[Contested]** = genuinely disputed · **[Theory]** = clinical-model claim without
outcome trials.*

---

## 0. The single most useful reframe

You have ~70% resolution of a right-sided thorax/shoulder pattern, residual right-shoulder
restriction, a stiff right upper-lateral rib zone, and *new* mild left-sided impingement-type
symptoms with a left-ER goal. The most important evidence-based fact for your whole plan:

> For rotator-cuff-related shoulder pain ("impingement"), **exercise/loading is first-line and
> works about as well as surgery** — including vs. *placebo* surgery — and the specifics of the
> mechanism (acromion shape, "pinching") are largely discredited. **[Established]**

That means: you do **not** need a perfect rib cage before you load. You need *graded, comfortable
loading* with attention to position, and the thorax/breathing work is an **adjunct that buys you
range and reduces compensation** — not a prerequisite gate. This single point should lower the
anxiety that often drives people in the PRI/Hartman space to endlessly "prepare" and never train.

## 1. Mechanism: four models, one phenomenon

### What each model is actually saying

**Conventional sports-medicine model**
- Your symptoms most likely map to **rotator-cuff-related shoulder pain (RCRSP)** — the modern
  umbrella term that replaced "subacromial impingement." The term changed because decompression
  surgery that shaves the acromion does **not** beat placebo surgery (CSAW, *Lancet* 2018;
  FIMPACT, *BMJ* 2018, confirmed at 5 yr), so the "bone pinches tendon" story collapsed. **[Established]**
- Associated findings: altered scapular motion (less upward rotation, less posterior tilt, less
  external rotation), higher upper-trap and lower serratus/lower-trap activity in symptomatic
  shoulders. **[Established as an association]** — but whether this is *cause, consequence, or
  compensation* is unresolved. **[Contested]**
- Pec-minor shortness correlates with the same scapular posture. **[Probable association]** /
  causation to pain **[Speculative]** (an RCT found adding pec-minor stretch gave no benefit over
  exercise alone).

**Bill Hartman / Zac Cupples model** (your therapist's framework)
- Sorts people by **infrasternal angle (ISA)** into "narrow" (inhalation/expansion-biased,
  ribs externally rotated) vs "wide" (exhalation/compression-biased) and prescribes breathing/
  reaching to restore the missing direction. The ISA landmark is real; the *typing system* has no
  published reliability/validity data. **[Theory]**
- Uses real rib physiology — **pump-handle** (upper ribs, A–P) vs **bucket-handle** (lower ribs,
  lateral) motion. **[Established physiology]**
- Signature claim: *"shoulder external rotation / flexion requires posterior (and region-specific)
  thorax expansion."* The **direction** has lab support — one study showed restricting thoracic
  expansion reduced scapulothoracic motion and forced compensatory glenohumeral motion during
  shoulder ER. **[Probable]** The **specific segmental map** (which vertebrae, which degrees) is
  proprietary and unvalidated. **[Theory]**

**PRI (Postural Restoration)**
- **Zone of apposition (ZOA)** — the region where the diaphragm is apposed to the inner lower rib
  cage — is **real, established respiratory physiology** (De Troyer & Estenne). **[Established]**
- **Diaphragm asymmetry** (larger/longer right crus, higher right dome under the liver) is **real
  anatomy**. **[Established]** The leap to *"therefore everyone has a Left-AIC/Right-BC pattern
  needing left-biased repositioning"* is the most-criticized, unvalidated part. **[Contested/Theory]**
- The famous "blow up a balloon" paper (Boyle 2010) is a **technique-description paper, not an
  efficacy trial** — it's routinely mis-cited as proof. **[Theory]**

**DNS (Dynamic Neuromuscular Stabilization / Prague School)**
- Uses developmental positions (bear, crawl, dead-bug) and **intra-abdominal pressure** regulation.
  The **diaphragm's postural/stabilizing role is well-established** (Kolář's MRI studies, 2010–2012;
  chronic-LBP patients show reduced diaphragm excursion under limb loading). **[Established mechanism /
  Probable clinical association]** The developmental framing is **[Theory]** built on solid physiology.

### Where they agree, differ, and overlap (a translation table)

| Model jargon | Conventional term | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Zone of apposition (PRI) | Diaphragm–ribcage apposition (De Troyer) | **Identical, established** |
| Compression/expansion strategy (Hartman) | Exhalation/inhalation bias; rib int/ext rotation | Repackaged real physiology |
| Pump-/bucket-handle | Same — standard rib kinematics | **Identical, established** |
| "Scapular dump" (Cupples/PRI) | Scapular anterior tilt + downward rotation | Jargon for a real posture |
| Sagittal stabilization (DNS) | Feedforward trunk co-contraction / IAP | Established mechanism |
| Left AIC / Right BC (PRI) | Stereotyped tri-planar asymmetry | **Proprietary, contested** |
| "Rib flare" inflating ER | Lumbar extension + anterior rib external rotation | Real coupling, established |

**Agreement (all four):** the scapula must sit and move on a mobile rib cage; thoracic position
changes shoulder mechanics; breathing/trunk control influences how the shoulder loads. These are
all **[Probable–Established]**.

**Genuine disagreement / weak evidence:** the proprietary *classification* systems (ISA typing,
Left-AIC/Right-BC) and the claim that drills produce **durable, measurable changes in rib-cage
shape/position** — that pivotal link is **unproven [Speculative]**. Breathing exercises *do* modestly
reduce pain and improve trunk control in trials, but with high risk of bias and low certainty, and
likely via **general** mechanisms, not the model-specific reasons claimed. **[Probable, low-certainty]**

**Bottom line on models:** Treat Hartman/PRI/DNS as a *useful movement vocabulary and a good
warm-up/positioning toolkit*, not as validated diagnostic science. The physiology underneath is
real; the specific cause-and-effect prescriptions are clinical theory. Your therapist's drills are
reasonable — just don't treat "fixing the pattern" as a prerequisite to training.

## 2. Left shoulder external rotation: differentiate before you treat

### What can limit ER, and the tell-tale signs

ER limiters are **position-dependent** (cadaveric sequential-cutting, Turkel 1981 **[Established]**):

| Where ER is limited | Likely structure |
|---|---|
| Arm **at side** | Subscapularis, superior GH ligament + coracohumeral ligament (rotator interval) |
| **45° abduction** | Middle GH ligament |
| **90° abduction** | Anterior band of inferior GH ligament + lower subscapularis |

So **where** you lose ER localizes the limiter. Then differentiate by *quality*:

- **Hard, capsular end-feel, passive ER also lost** → think capsule/adhesive capsulitis.
  *Important branch point:* frozen shoulder's hallmark is **lost passive ER at the side**, usual age
  **40–65**, strongly linked to **diabetes**. At 30 this is atypical — but genuine hard-end-feel
  passive-ER loss warrants a clinician. **[Established]**
- **Full passive ER, limited only actively / under load, springy end-feel that eases after an
  isometric** → muscle stiffness, **protective guarding**, or motor-control — the most likely picture
  for you given your history. **[Probable]**
- **"Anterior humeral glide"** (Sahrmann) is a popular label, but measurable humeral translation in
  painful shoulders is only millimetres and inconsistent; the better-evidenced kinematic story is
  **scapular** (posterior tilt/upward rotation), influenced by thoracic posture. Use it as a coaching
  cue, not a diagnosis. **[Contested]**
- **Measurement artifact:** in 90/90, lumbar arch + rib flare add fake "ER" degrees. **[Established
  coupling]** This is exactly the compensation you're trying to avoid.

### How to self-assess (and what to watch for)

Test ER in **three positions** and compare left vs right:
1. **ER at side** (elbow pinned to ribs, neutral) — isolates interval/subscapularis.
2. **ER at 45° abduction** — middle GHL.
3. **ER at 90/90** — inferior GHL; most relevant to pressing/overhead.

Do them **supine with the scapula supported and trunk neutral first** (removes trunk
compensation), then **standing** — if standing range is much worse, you're looking at **guarding/
motor-control**, not true tissue restriction. **[Probable]**

**Compensations that mean "you just cheated the rep":** rib flare, lumbar arch, the humeral head
jutting forward, scapula tipping forward ("dump"), neck extension/shrug, or pinch/pain. If you see
these, you've left the true glenohumeral range.

### Matching the fix to the limitation — and a critical don't

- **DON'T** use the **sleeper stretch or cross-body stretch for ER** — those improve **internal**
  rotation and are for posterior tightness. Misapplying them is one of the most common errors.
  **[Established directionality]**
- **For anterior/interval restriction:** open-book / doorway-style anterior-chest and gentle
  subscapularis-directed work.
- **For muscle stiffness or guarding (your likely case):** **loaded end-range ER and eccentrics**
  raise both tolerance and fascicle length — much of "mobility" gain is increased **stretch
  tolerance**, not structural lengthening, which is exactly why graded loading into range works.
  **[Established mechanism]** **Contract-relax (PNF)** improves shoulder ER ROM specifically. **[Probable]**
- **Positioning/breathing (your therapist's lane):** stacking ribs over pelvis (full exhale, posterior
  pelvic tilt) to kill the rib-flare/lumbar-extension artifact is **mechanistically sound for getting
  a true ER reading and reducing compensation**, though outcome evidence is weak. **[Theory/Probable]**

**Practical takeaway for your left ER:** because it's recent, mild, and you have a guarding history,
bias toward **loaded, comfortable end-range ER (cable/band ER at side and at 90/90), light eccentrics,
and contract-relax**, *with the rib cage stacked so you measure real range* — rather than aggressive
passive stretching.

## 3. The stiff right upper-lateral rib (~6" below armpit)

That location is the **serratus anterior / lateral intercostal / external oblique / lat interface** on
the bucket-handle (lateral-expansion) ribs. It's the exact region that has to **expand laterally and
posteriorly** for the scapula to upwardly rotate and the arm to flex/externally rotate.
Restricting thoracic expansion measurably reduces scapulothoracic motion. **[Probable]**

### Interventions ranked by likely value (for *this* problem)

**Higher value — directly drive lateral/posterior rib expansion + serratus on a reaching scapula:**
- **Side-lying rib/lateral-expansion breathing** (top arm reaching overhead) — most directly targets
  that bucket-handle zone. **[Theory, mechanistically strong]**
- **Quadruped rock-back breathing** and **all-fours reach** — protraction + posterior/lateral rib
  expansion; serratus loaded in a closed chain. Closed-chain work avoids the serratus/upper-trap
  imbalance seen open-chain. **[Probable]**
- **Bear position breathing/hover** — same as above with more serratus demand.
- **Serratus wall slides** — strong serratus EMG with low upper-trap ratio. **[Established EMG]**
- **Cable/landmine press *with reach* (full protraction at lockout)** — lets the scapula travel onto
  the rib cage; the reach is the part that matters for your rib zone. **[Probable]**

**Moderate / situational:**
- **One-arm cable row with reach** — the *reach* phase (protraction) drives serratus/expansion; the
  pull phase is fine but isn't the rib-mobility driver.
- **Loaded carries** — good for rib-position-under-load and serratus endurance; less of a targeted
  mobility tool. **[Probable]**

**Lower value for rib expansion specifically:**
- **Manual therapy / self-mobilization (lacrosse ball, rib mobs):** can give **short-term** relief and
  may make the area feel less guarded so you can breathe into it, but effects are transient and it's
  an **adjunct, not a fix**. **[Probable, short-term only]**

**Best sequence:** soften/relax the area (breathing ± brief manual) → then *actively* expand it under a
reaching scapula (quadruped/bear reach, serratus slide) → then *load* the new range (landmine/cable
press with reach, carries). Mobility you don't load, you lose.

## 4. Scapula-on-thorax mechanics (what "good" looks like)

Normal arm elevation = **scapulohumeral rhythm ≈ 2:1** overall, but it's **non-linear** (steeper
early "setting phase," ~2.3–2.7:1 above 30°). At full elevation the scapula **upwardly rotates
~50–60°, posteriorly tilts ~20–30°, externally rotates**. **[Established]** A kyphotic/protracted
resting thorax pushes the scapula into anterior tilt and *reduces* all three — narrowing space and
reducing available ER. **[Probable]**

By task:
- **Flexion/overhead:** needs the full upward-rotation + posterior-tilt + thoracic-extension package.
  Serratus + lower trap + upper trap form the **force couple**. (Note: the upper trap is **not** a
  villain — it attaches to the clavicle and drives scapular external rotation; "demonizing" it is a
  mischaracterization. **[Established]**)
- **Pressing (bench):** the powerlifting setup **deliberately pins the scapula retracted/depressed**,
  which *suppresses* serratus and protraction — fine for max strength, but it's why bench does little
  for your serratus/rib goals. **Push-ups let the scapula protract freely** → far more serratus. **[Probable]**
- **Rowing/pulling:** drives retraction + posterior tilt (mid/lower trap, rhomboids). Good — **unless**
  you let it become lat-dominant lumbar-extension hauling (see §5).
- **Landmine press:** mid-range, scapular-plane, allows upward rotation with reduced cuff/AC demand —
  an ideal bridge between rehab and pressing. **[Probable, mechanistic]**
- **Load changes rhythm:** heavier loads alter scapular contribution by phase. **[Established]** — so
  re-load gradually.

*Caveat worth holding:* **scapular "dyskinesis" is present in ~42% of *asymptomatic* athletes** and
isn't reliably more common in painful shoulders; "scapular stabilization" as a concept is critiqued
(the scapula is *meant to move*). Don't chase a perfect-looking scapula — chase comfortable, full,
loadable motion. **[Contested]**

## 5. Exercise categories for your return

### Best early-stage pressing (free scapula, scapular-plane, reduced end-range stress)
| Exercise | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| **Push-up on handles / feet-elevated push-up** (you already do these) | Closed-chain, free scapular protraction → serratus; you control depth/range. **[Established serratus benefit]** |
| **Incline push-up** | Regression; same benefits, less load |
| **Neutral-grip DB floor press** | Floor caps end-range shoulder extension; neutral grip lowers anterior stress. **[Probable]** |
| **Cable press with reach** | Adds the protraction/serratus reach you need for the rib zone |
| **Half-kneeling landmine press** (you do this pattern) | Scapular-plane, upward-rotation-friendly, low cuff demand. **[Probable]** |
| **Neutral-grip machine press / low-incline DB press** | Constrained path, neutral grip, scapular-plane |

**Why neutral grip + scapular plane + reduced abduction:** musculoskeletal modeling shows **wider grip
and greater abduction increase glenohumeral/AC joint load**; neutral, narrower, scapular-plane lowers
it. **[Probable]**

### Pressing to delay (and the criteria to earn them back)
| Exercise | Concern | Reintroduce when… |
|---|---|---|
| **Barbell bench (esp. wide grip)** | Fixed bar path + abduction = highest shoulder torque; shoulder is the top bench injury site | Pain-free pressing 8–10 wks; keep grip ≤1.5× biacromial width **[Probable]** |
| **Heavy overhead press** | Demands full upward rotation + thoracic ext you're rebuilding | Full pain-free overhead ROM, solid landmine pressing first |
| **Dips** | Deep extension/IR loads anterior capsule (evidence weak but plausible) | Late; only if pain-free and not provocative **[Contested]** |
| **Wide-grip incline** | Max abduction + anterior stress | After bench tolerated |
| **Behind-the-neck press** | Forced ER + horizontal abduction on anterior capsule | Optional at best; skip unless mobility is excellent **[Contested]** |
| **Upright row** | IR + elevation = the one position most likely to provoke | Keep elbows **below 90°**, wide grip, or just substitute. **[Probable]** |

### Best pulling (and which reinforce bad habits)
**Good scapular motion, low compensation risk:**
- **Chest-supported row / seal row** — comparable lat EMG to bent-over rows but **far less erector/
  lumbar load** → can't cheat into lumbar extension. **Best choice for you.** **[Probable]**
- **One-arm cable row with reach** (you do this) — the reach restores protraction/serratus; excellent.
- **Half-kneeling / neutral-grip pulldown** — controlled, scapular-plane, front (not behind-neck).
- **TRX row** — closed-chain, self-limiting.

**Use with care (can reinforce lat-dominant / extension / neck patterns):**
- **One-arm DB row** — fine, but easy to turn into lumbar-rotation hauling; keep it strict.
- **Heavy lat-dominant pulldowns/pull-ups:** the **lat is the prime internal rotator + extensor of
  the humerus [Established anatomy]** — heavy lat-dominant pulling adds an IR/extension moment and
  encourages lumbar extension to finish range. Your **typewriter pull-up and dragon-roll-to-flag**
  (current program) are advanced and lat/extension-heavy — keep volume modest and **watch for rib
  flare / neck tone**; regress if the left shoulder complains.
- **Behind-neck pulldown** — skip (forced ER/abduction, no upside). **[Probable]**

## 6. Warm-up: what's worth doing, and two ready-to-use sequences

**Honest framing:** evidence that bespoke breathing/movement-prep drills **improve subsequent lifting
performance** is weak. **[Speculative]** Their justified roles are: (a) restoring *true* range and
reducing compensation, (b) grooving scapular/rib motion, (c) raising tissue temperature. Brief
stretching (<60 s/muscle) does **not** hurt strength — only long holds do. **[Established]** So keep
warm-ups short and purposeful; don't let them become the workout.

Drill value for *your* goals:
- **High value:** side-lying rib breathing, quadruped rock-back, bear hover, serratus reach/wall
  slide, cable reach — all drive the rib-expansion + serratus + scapular-motion you specifically need.
- **Solid general core/IAP:** 90/90 hip-lift breathing (± reach), dead bug — good for stacking ribs
  over pelvis and feedforward trunk control. **[Probable]**
- **Situational:** crawling (fun serratus/contralateral integration), loaded carries (better as a
  finisher than a warm-up). Plain wall slides — fine, lower ceiling than the above.

### Upper-body day warm-up (~10 min)
1. 90/90 hip-lift breathing with reach — 4 breaths (stack ribs over pelvis) · ~2 min
2. Side-lying right rib-cage expansion breathing (top arm reach) — 5 breaths/side, **bias the stiff
   right side** · ~2 min
3. Quadruped rock-back with reach (round upper back, push the floor away) — 6 reps · ~1.5 min
4. Serratus wall slide or bear hover — 8 reps · ~1.5 min
5. Banded/cable ER at side + at 90/90 — 10 each, **left-ER focus** · ~2 min
6. Light landmine or cable press w/ reach to "find" the pattern — 1–2 ramp sets · ~1 min

### Lower-body day warm-up (~8–10 min)
1. 90/90 hip-lift breathing — 4 breaths · ~1.5 min
2. Dead bug (slow, exhale fully, ribs down — no flare) — 6/side · ~1.5 min
3. Half-kneeling adductor/hip rock + cross-connect reach (your Day-1 A–D theme) · ~2 min
4. Reverse lunge to cross-connect / high-step carioca (already your warm-up) · ~2 min
5. Goblet-squat or leg-press ramp sets · ~2 min

## 7. A phased 12-week return-to-lifting plan

**Principles baked in:** progressive volume (~10+ hard sets/muscle/week is the dose-response sweet
spot **[Established]**); train close-ish to failure; protein **1.6–2.2 g/kg/day [Established]**; you're
a returning lifter so **recomposition (lose fat + gain muscle) is realistic** and retraining is faster
than starting fresh **[Probable]** (the "muscle memory" *mechanism* is contested, the *fast comeback*
is not). Use the **traffic-light pain rule** below throughout. Keep your current Cupples warm-up
blocks (A–D: ER low oblique sit, frog breathing, seated upper-back expansion, reverse-lunge cross-
connect, high-step carioca) — they're well-aligned with this plan.

**Traffic-light pain monitoring [Established for tendinopathy/load mgmt]:**
- 🟢 0–3/10 during, settles by next morning → continue/progress.
- 🟡 4–5/10, settles within 24 h → hold load, don't progress, reassess.
- 🔴 >5/10, lingers >24 h, night pain, or new weakness → back off, see §Red flags.

### Phase 1 — Weeks 1–3: Re-groove & reposition (low load, high quality)
- **Goal:** full *comfortable* range, scapular/rib motion, reconnect patterns, zero flare-ups.
- **Pressing:** push-ups on handles, incline push-ups, neutral-grip DB floor press, cable press w/
  reach. 2–3 sets, leave 3–4 reps in reserve (RIR).
- **Pulling:** chest-supported row, one-arm cable row **with reach**, neutral pulldown. 2–3 sets.
- **Direct ER/cuff:** cable/band ER at side + 90/90 (left focus), side-lying ER. 2×12–15.
- **Lower:** keep your seated hack / pendulum / leg-press / single-leg RDL work — lower body has no
  restriction, train it normally.
- **Volume:** ~6–8 sets/muscle/week upper. **Tempo controlled, full range, no compensation.**

### Phase 2 — Weeks 4–6: Build capacity
- Add **half-kneeling landmine press**, **low-incline DB press (neutral)**, **seal/CSR rows** heavier.
- Progress ER work to **loaded eccentrics** + contract-relax for left ER.
- Volume → ~8–12 sets/muscle/week; RIR 2–3; begin progressive overload (add reps then load).
- **Reintroduction check before Phase 3:** pain-free full-ROM pressing, left ER within ~5–10° of
  right, no rib flare under load, no next-day soreness in the shoulder.

### Phase 3 — Weeks 7–9: Hypertrophy emphasis
- Pressing: low-incline & flat **neutral/DB** work heavier; landmine press loaded; *optionally*
  reintroduce **barbell bench at ≤1.5× biacromial grip** if all Phase-2 criteria met.
- Pulling: full menu (add strict one-arm DB row, weighted pulldown) — keep ribs stacked, no flare.
- Volume → 12–16 sets/muscle/week; RIR 1–2 on isolation work; **deload around week 9** (½ volume).
- Keep 5–8 min of the rib/serratus/ER work as a permanent warm-up — that's how you *keep* the range.

### Phase 4 — Weeks 10–12: Reintegrate & test the ceiling
- Cautiously trial the "delayed" lifts **only if symptom-free**: barbell bench (controlled), heavier
  DB overhead → barbell overhead press once full pain-free overhead ROM + upward rotation confirmed.
  **Still skip behind-neck pressing and upright rows** (poor risk/reward).
- Volume individualized 12–18 sets/muscle/week; full progressive overload; periodic deloads.
- **Maintenance forever:** 1–2× /week of side-lying rib breathing + serratus reach + left-ER loading,
  and *audit your big lifts for rib flare / lumbar extension / neck shrug* — those are your personal
  "you're compensating again" signals.

**On your current program:** it's well-constructed and consistent with everything above — it already
front-loads expansion/ER/cross-connect work and uses reach-biased pressing and rows. The few things to
watch: **typewriter pull-ups, dragon-roll-to-flag, and tricep dips** are the most shoulder/extension-
demanding items — treat them as Phase-3/4 movements, keep volume modest, and be the first to regress
them if the left shoulder or right rib zone protests. Everything else you can run now.

## Red flags — stop and see a clinician (ideally back to your PT/MD)

- **Significant trauma + sudden weakness / can't actively lift the arm** → possible cuff tear/fracture.
- **Constant, unremitting night pain** unrelated to position, **with fever/chills/unexplained weight
  loss or cancer history** → needs medical work-up.
- **Progressive global stiffness with hard loss of passive ER** (esp. if diabetic) → adhesive
  capsulitis — atypical at 30 but exactly the picture that needs a hands-on exam.
- **Pain radiating below the elbow, numbness/tingling in a dermatome, grip weakness** → possible
  **cervical** source (C5–C6), not shoulder.
- **Visible muscle wasting or marked atraumatic weakness** → possible nerve involvement → neuro referral.
- Any **left** symptom that escalates from "occasional mild pinch" to **consistent pain, ROM loss, or
  weakness** — get it looked at before pushing load; new/worsening is the signal, not stable/mild.

## The honest evidence summary

- **Strongest ground you're standing on:** loading/exercise is first-line and as good as surgery for
  RCRSP; protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg and ~10+ sets/muscle/week drive recomposition; traffic-light pain
  management lets you train *through* mild symptoms safely; brief warm-up stretching is harmless.
- **Reasonable, mechanism-backed but not RCT-proven:** neutral-grip/scapular-plane/landmine/floor-
  press modifications; chest-supported rows to kill lumbar compensation; thoracic expansion → scapular
  motion → shoulder ROM chain; serratus/lower-trap exercise selection.
- **Useful vocabulary, weak outcome evidence — adjuncts, not gates:** ISA typing, Left-AIC/Right-BC,
  the specific PRI/Hartman breathing prescriptions, and the claim that drills permanently reshape the
  rib cage. They likely help via general mechanisms (range, compensation, trunk control) — so use them,
  but don't let "fixing the pattern" postpone training you're cleared to do.
- **Don't do:** sleeper/cross-body stretch for an *ER* deficit (those are IR tools); behind-neck
  pressing; upright rows above 90°; chasing a "perfect" scapula.
